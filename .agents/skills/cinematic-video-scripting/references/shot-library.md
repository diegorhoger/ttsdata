# Shot Library and Visual Grammar

Use this as a decision library, not a checklist. Select shots because they serve the story.

## Shot scale

1. **Wide** — establishes environment, geography, scale, isolation, or context.
2. **Medium** — balances person/action with environment; strong default for dialogue and demonstrations.
3. **Close-up** — emphasizes face, emotion, product, or important action.
4. **Macro / extreme detail** — reveals texture, tiny mechanisms, makeup detail, ingredients, interfaces, or tactile proof.
5. **Wide-angle close-up** — intimate, energetic, distorted proximity; useful for strong hooks, comedy, or subjective intensity.
6. **Fisheye** — conspicuous distortion and stylization; use deliberately, rarely.
7. **Fisheye close-up** — exaggerated intimacy/comedy/surrealism; never a neutral default.

## Subject orientation

8. **Profile** — emphasizes direction, contemplation, silhouette, or interaction outside frame.
9. **Three-quarter** — dimensional and natural for beauty, interviews, product holding, and conversational delivery.
10. **Rear** — follows the subject, withholds identity/information, or places viewer behind action.
11. **Over-the-shoulder** — establishes relationships, screens, conversations, POV-adjacent demonstrations.
12. **POV** — makes the viewer experience the subject's perspective.
13. **Selfie** — immediacy, intimacy, UGC authenticity, confession, reaction.

## Camera height and angle

14. **Eye-level** — neutral, direct, trustworthy, conversational.
15. **Low-angle** — can communicate scale, confidence, dominance, aspiration, or drama.
16. **High-angle** — can reveal layout/context or make a subject feel smaller/vulnerable; do not use mechanically.
17. **Dutch angle** — communicates imbalance, tension, weirdness, disruption; use sparingly.
18. **Overhead / top-down** — ideal for layouts, food, beauty products, unboxing, hands, processes, spatial clarity.
19. **Ground-level** — emphasizes footsteps, objects, scale, entrances, speed, or unusual perspective.
20. **Worm's-eye view** — extreme low perspective for height, scale, surrealism, or dramatic reveals.
21. **Aerial / bird's-eye** — establishes geography, pattern, scale, journey, or location.
22. **Inside-a-hole / enclosed POV** — viewer looks outward from a container/opening; strong reveal or object-perspective device.
23. **Hole-level / aperture-level** — frames action through an opening at its level; useful for discovery and transitions.
24. **Inside-the-fridge / cabinet / bag / drawer** — object-space POV for reveals, routines, product retrieval, comedy, and transitions.

## Optical and framing devices

25. **Prism reflection** — fragments or duplicates imagery for dreamlike, beauty, transition, or conceptual shots.
26. **Silhouette** — emphasizes shape, mystery, transformation, mood, or reveal.
27. **Foreground occlusion** — partially blocks frame with an object/person to create depth, voyeurism, reveal, or transition.
28. **Foreground focus** — foreground subject/object is sharp while background provides context.
29. **Background focus** — foreground intentionally soft while background carries the story; useful for reveals and attention transfer.
30. **Lens flare** — atmospheric highlight or transition device; avoid when it obscures necessary information.
31. **Magnifying glass / optical insert** — literal or stylized emphasis on a detail; useful for inspection, humor, or educational explanation.

## Additional production-essential shots

The source video's list is useful but incomplete. Also consider:

32. **Extreme wide / establishing shot** — stronger environmental context than a normal wide.
33. **Extreme close-up** — eyes, lips, button presses, texture, micro-expression.
34. **Two-shot / group shot** — communicates relationships and reactions.
35. **Insert shot** — isolates an object or action required for comprehension.
36. **Cutaway** — temporarily leaves the primary action to show reaction, context, evidence, or conceal an edit.
37. **Reaction shot** — captures emotional response; essential for human storytelling.
38. **Tracking / follow shot** — moves with the subject and creates momentum.
39. **Push-in** — increases emphasis, intimacy, realization, or tension.
40. **Pull-out** — reveals context, creates emotional distance, or delivers a visual punchline.
41. **Pan / tilt** — reveals information spatially; use because the viewer needs to discover something.
42. **Orbit / arc** — creates dimensionality around a person/product; strong for hero reveals.
43. **Locked-off static** — stability, honesty, deadpan comedy, comparison, tutorial clarity.
44. **Handheld** — immediacy and human presence; should feel intentional rather than merely shaky.
45. **Rack focus** — transfers attention between depth planes without cutting.
46. **Whip pan / motion transition** — energetic transition when direction and motion continuity support it.
47. **Reveal shot** — composes an obstruction/movement so new information enters deliberately.
48. **Match cut** — connects shots through shape, action, framing, color, or meaning.
49. **Screen / device capture** — use true screen recording when legibility matters rather than filming an unreadable screen.
50. **Before/after matched frame** — same framing, lighting, scale, and conditions when making legitimate visual comparisons.

## Selection heuristics

Choose shots by narrative function:

- **Orient:** extreme wide, wide, aerial, overhead.
- **Connect:** eye-level medium, three-quarter, selfie, two-shot.
- **Emphasize:** close-up, extreme close-up, macro, push-in.
- **Demonstrate:** medium, overhead, POV, insert, screen capture.
- **Prove:** macro, insert, matched comparison, locked-off demonstration.
- **Reveal:** rear, foreground occlusion, rack focus, pull-out, inside-object POV.
- **Intensify:** low-angle, wide-angle close-up, handheld, push-in.
- **Destabilize:** Dutch, fisheye, unusual aperture POV.
- **Beautify:** macro, three-quarter, prism, silhouette, controlled flare, orbit.
- **Transition:** whip pan, occlusion, match cut, movement across lens.
- **Resolve:** stable medium/close-up, pull-out, hero shot, contextual wide.

## Retention without chaos

Visual change is not synonymous with a cut. Retention can come from:
- subject movement
- camera movement
- reframing
- focus change
- prop interaction
- text reveal
- lighting change
- new evidence
- location change
- sound transition

Do not cut simply because a timer says to cut.

## Continuity safeguards

When generating or capturing sequential shots, preserve:
- identity and facial features
- hair/makeup
- wardrobe
- product packaging and labels
- hand/object orientation
- environment geometry
- lighting direction and time
- camera-side/screen direction
- state changes caused by prior actions

For AI-generated footage, explicitly repeat critical continuity anchors across prompts.

## Ethical visual proof

If a shot is used as evidence, preserve comparable conditions. Do not change lighting, focal length, pose, exposure, filters, or framing in ways that manufacture an apparent result. Clearly identify simulations or illustrative AI-generated footage when context requires it.
